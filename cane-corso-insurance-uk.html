Cane Corso Insurance in the UK: Vet Fees vs Third-Party Liability Exclusions

Why many British policies treat the Italian guardian breed differently, which part of the cover goes missing first, and the old-fashioned checks that still work.

Home›Blog›Cane Corso Insurance UK

Published: 2026-10-11 · Updated: 2026-10-11 · Cane Corso World

Finding Cane Corso insurance in the UK is harder than finding a policy for a Labrador, and the reason is rarely the vet bill: it is the third-party liability section that insurers drop first, or the whole breed that lands on an exclusion list. Owners who have kept guardian breeds for decades follow the same proven routine — read the exclusions before the price, and never assume the liability box is ticked. This guide walks through that routine using the insurers’ own published documents.

Quick answer: several mainstream UK pet insurers publish documents that name the Cane Corso as a breed they will not insure at all, and the one free liability route many owners relied on — Dogs Trust membership — stops including insurance for renewals from 1 July 2026. Plan vet-fee cover and liability cover as two separate jobs.

Why is Cane Corso insurance in the UK so hard to arrange?

Insurers price and accept dogs by breed, and the Cane Corso sits in the group of large mastiff and guardian types that underwriters treat cautiously. The breed itself has a long working heritage: the PDSA describes it as a mastiff type thought to descend from a Roman war dog, standing 58–70 cm and weighing 40–50 kg, with a lifespan of under 10 years. A dog that size can cause serious damage in a single incident, which is exactly what a liability underwriter is paid to worry about.

The result is not a single “Cane Corso rule” across the market. Each insurer writes its own list, and the lists differ in two important ways: some refuse the breed outright, while others accept it for vet fees but strip out third-party liability.

Second, several documents exclude dogs used for guarding or security — the Agria IPID excludes “the use of your pet for guarding, security or racing” — which matters for a breed whose traditional job was exactly that.

Vet fees vs third-party liability: what is the difference?

A standard UK dog policy bundles two very different promises:

  • Vet fees pay for treatment of your dog’s illness or injury, up to an annual or per-condition limit.
  • Third-party liability pays compensation and legal costs if someone else is injured or their property damaged by your dog and you are found legally responsible.

That last row is the time-proven reason experienced owners treat liability as the non-negotiable half. A vet bill is painful but bounded by what you agree to spend; a court award for an injured person is not.

How big can Cane Corso vet bills get?

The Association of British Insurers reported that members paid a record £1.23 billion in pet claims in 2024, with dogs accounting for £933 million and an average claim of £685. The same release notes that elbow dysplasia treatment, a common lameness problem in large dogs, can cost over £50,000.

That is relevant here because the PDSA lists hip dysplasia, elbow dysplasia, gastric dilatation volvulus (GDV) and eye problems such as entropion, ectropion and cherry eye as concerns for the breed. For an owner self-funding part of the vet risk, those rules make an old habit — asking for the estimate in writing — a legal right.

What does the law say about liability if your dog injures someone?

British dog law is old and well tested, and two statutes matter most:

Animals Act 1971 (civil liability)

Under section 2(2) of the Animals Act 1971, the keeper of an animal not of a dangerous species can be liable for damage it causes when the damage was likely or likely to be severe, that likelihood came from characteristics not normal for the species (or normal only at particular times or circumstances), and the keeper knew of those characteristics. This is the kind of claim third-party liability insurance exists to pay.

Dangerous Dogs Act 1991 (criminal offence)

Section 3 of the Dangerous Dogs Act 1991 makes it an offence for a dog to be dangerously out of control anywhere in England and Wales, public or private, with a householder exception for trespassers inside a dwelling. If a person is injured the offence is aggravated, carrying up to 5 years’ imprisonment on indictment, or up to 14 years if a person dies. Insurance does not make this risk go away: the Dogs Trust cover, for example, states that fines, compensation or penalties ordered after a criminal prosecution are not covered.

Dogs Trust liability cover: what changed in 2026?

For years, Dogs Trust Companion Club membership was the traditional low-cost answer for owners of breeds that pet insurers would not cover for liability. According to the charity’s Companion Club benefits page, the cover provides up to £1,000,000 per claim, with a £200 excess in the UK and £500 in the Republic of Ireland.

The key change: the page states the insurance benefit is only available to supporters with a renewal date up to and including 30 June 2026, and anyone renewing from 1 July 2026 will not have insurance included in their next subscription. If your current membership started before that cut-off, the cover runs to the end of that membership year — so diarize the end date now.

The benefit also had exclusions worth knowing even while it lasts: it does not cover injury to anyone who normally lives in your home, incidents involving a dog used for trade or profession, your own dog’s vet fees, or dogs belonging to other people that you look after.

How to cover the liability gap for a Cane Corso

With breed exclusions on the pet side and the Dogs Trust route closing, owners are back to the methods that pre-date comparison websites:

  1. Call your home insurer and ask in writing whether its personal or animal liability section covers a named Cane Corso. Do not rely on a general “pets covered” line — ask for the breed to be confirmed.
  2. Ask specialist brokers who write standalone dog liability, and read the eligibility sheet before paying. The petGuard document above shows why: it names the breed as ineligible and excludes dogs with any complaint history.
  3. Disclose honestly. Every document we read conditions cover on accurate breed and behavior information; a policy voided after a claim is worse than no policy.
  4. Keep the paper trail — training records, microchip details and written confirmations. They are the evidence an insurer or court will ask for.

How should UK owners separate vet-fee cover from liability cover?

The most useful first step is to split the policy into two questions. Vet-fee cover is about illness, injury, diagnostics, surgery, medication, referral care, and follow-up treatment for the dog. Third-party liability is about damage or injury the dog causes to someone else or their property. A Cane Corso owner can be declined, restricted, or priced differently in either area, so do not treat one positive quote as proof that the whole risk is covered.

Read the exclusions in the same order every time: breed wording, behaviour wording, public-place conditions, lead and muzzle requirements, household exclusions, professional-use exclusions, and claim-notification deadlines. If the insurer uses phrases such as dangerous dogs, guard dogs, business use, behavioural incidents, or deliberate acts, ask how those phrases apply to your actual dog and household. What should you document before choosing a policy?

Keep a clean file before you buy, not only after a claim. Save the quote date, insurer name, policy wording, excess, annual limit, vet-fee limit, liability limit, waiting periods, breed notes, and any written answer from the insurer. If you ask about Cane Corso eligibility by phone, follow up in writing and save the reply. A verbal reassurance is harder to rely on than a clear policy email or chat transcript.

For vet-fee cover, document pre-existing conditions, previous investigations, medication history, and any referral letters. For liability cover, document training, secure fencing, walking arrangements, who handles the dog, and whether the dog is used for guarding, breeding, paid work, or protection. The point is not to make the dog look perfect. The point is to avoid surprises when an insurer later asks what was disclosed. How do exclusions change the real value of a quote?

A low monthly price can be misleading if the policy excludes the scenario you are most worried about. Vet-fee exclusions may matter if the dog has previous orthopaedic, skin, digestive, airway, or behavioural notes. Liability exclusions may matter if the policy restricts certain breeds, incidents away from home, dogs handled by someone outside the household, or events connected with business activity. The value of the quote is the covered risk, not the headline premium.

Frequently asked questions about Cane Corso insurance in the UK

Can you get pet insurance for a Cane Corso in the UK?

Sometimes, but not from every insurer. Agria, Lassie, Puffin and petGuard all publish documents naming the Cane Corso as a breed they do not cover, so you need to check each insurer’s current breed list before applying.

Is the Cane Corso a banned breed in the UK?

No. The GOV.UK banned dogs list names the Pit Bull Terrier, Japanese Tosa, Dogo Argentino, Fila Brasileiro and XL Bully, not the Cane Corso. GOV.UK notes that a banned type is judged by what a dog looks like rather than its breed name, and section 3 of the Dangerous Dogs Act 1991 applies to every dog that is dangerously out of control.

Does Dogs Trust still include third-party liability insurance?

Only for supporters whose membership renewed on or before 30 June 2026. Dogs Trust states that anyone renewing from 1 July 2026 will not have insurance included in their next subscription.

What is the difference between vet fee cover and third-party liability?

Vet fee cover pays for treating your own dog. Third-party liability pays compensation and legal costs if your dog injures another person or damages their property and you are found legally responsible.

Does liability insurance pay criminal fines under the Dangerous Dogs Act?

No. The Dogs Trust policy wording, for example, excludes fines, compensation or penalties ordered by a court after a criminal prosecution. Liability insurance responds to civil claims, not criminal penalties.

Is a Cane Corso cross treated differently by insurers?

Usually not. The Agria, Puffin and petGuard documents all extend their exclusions to crosses or mixed breeds of the named breeds.

The bottom line on Cane Corso insurance in the UK

The traditional advice still holds because it still works: treat vet fees and liability as two separate problems, read the breed list before the price, and get every confirmation in writing. For most owners, Cane Corso insurance in the UK now means a vet-fee policy (or a disciplined savings fund) plus a liability solution confirmed by name — and with the Dogs Trust benefit ending at your next renewal after June 2026, the liability half is the one to fix first.
