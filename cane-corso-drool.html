How Much Do Cane Corsos Drool? Owner Reports, Jowl Types and Fixes for the Water Bowl Mess

How much Cane Corsos drool surprises a lot of first-time owners. On the American Kennel Club's five-point drooling scale the breed sits in the middle, at 3. Corso homes still talk about towels in every room, slime in the water bowl and drool flung up to the ceiling. This guide puts the breed's score next to its mastiff relatives, explains what the lips have to do with it, and lists the fixes owners actually use at the water bowl.

Quick answer: the AKC rates the Cane Corso 3 out of 5 for drooling: "average", next to the Rottweiler, Boxer and Bullmastiff. It sits below the Mastiff (4) and well below the Neapolitan Mastiff and Dogue de Bordeaux (5). Most Corso drool is tied to drinking, food, heat and excitement, so the water station is where a few changes save the most cleaning.

How much do Cane Corsos drool compared with other mastiff breeds?

Less than most of their mastiff cousins. The AKC gives the Cane Corso 3 out of 5, the same as the Rottweiler, Boxer and Bullmastiff. The Mastiff scores 4, and the Neapolitan Mastiff and Dogue de Bordeaux score 5.

The AKC scale runs from "Less Likely to Drool" at 1, through "Average" at 3, to "Always Have a Towel" at 5. The AKC describes it as how drool-prone a breed tends to be, and warns neat freaks about "ropes of slobber on your arm or big wet spots on your clothes." The "what it means at home" column is our summary, not AKC wording. A breed score is an average for the breed. Individual Corsos vary a lot, which is why some owners say their dog barely drips while others clean the walls every week.

Why the score feels too low to some owners

A score of 3 describes how often a dog drools, not how much water it carries. A big dog with deep lips can drain a cup of water from its mouth onto the floor in one walk away from the bowl. Owners who came from a Labrador or a shepherd breed notice that jump first, even though the Corso is not a "5" breed.

What do the Cane Corso's lips have to do with drool?

A lot. The breed standard asks for upper lips that hang moderately and cover the lower jaw, which leaves a pocket where water and saliva collect before they spill.

The FCI breed standard for the Cane Corso says the upper lips, seen from the front, "form an inverted 'U' at their meeting point". Seen from the side, they hang moderately. "They cover the lower jaw and determine the profile of the lower part of the muzzle." The same standard calls for a muzzle that is "as broad as it is long" and deep from the side. Wide, deep and moderately lipped is a good description of a water scoop.

Three jowl types you will see in real Corsos

  • Tight lips: the upper lip just covers the lower jaw and the corner of the mouth sits high. These dogs mostly drip after drinking and dry off quickly.
  • Standard, moderate hang: the lip forms the clear inverted U from the front and folds over the lower jaw at the side. Expect a wet chin after water and strings when food is around.
  • Loose or heavy flews: the lip hangs below the jawline and the corner of the mouth forms a pocket. More water stays in the pocket after a drink, and head shakes throw it farther. This look sits further from the standard's "moderately", and it is more common in heavier lines and some mixes.

These categories are an owner's eye guide, not official terms. If you are choosing a puppy and drool matters to you, look at the lips of both parents, not just the puppy. Lips and muzzle fill out as the head grows, a process our adult size prediction guide follows month by month.

When do Cane Corsos drool the most?

Owners point to the same triggers again and again: right after drinking, when food is being prepared or eaten, in hot weather or after exercise, and when the dog is excited.

The American Standard Dog Training clip above lists the same set. Owners in Cane Corso communities add one more trigger: the head shake. A wet dog that shakes after drinking or eating is the source of most of the drool found on walls, doors and ceilings. That is why many owners do a weekly "look up" clean, not just a floor wipe.

What actually fixes the water bowl mess?

No single product stops it. What works is a small system: something to slow the splash, something to catch it, and a habit that dries the face before the dog walks away.

The slime in the bowl

Owners describe the same thing: after the first drink from fresh water, the bowl is full of slime again. Keeping two bowls and swapping them, rather than refilling one, means a clean bowl is always on the floor. Rinse and scrub the used one with hot water each day so the slick film does not build up on the sides.

How do you teach a Cane Corso to wipe its face?

Teach the dog to push its chin into a towel on cue, then ask for it every time it lifts its head from the bowl. Most of the training is done at the towel, not at the bowl.

  1. Pick a fixed spot. Hang a towel on a low hook, or drape it over a box or low bin next to the water bowl, at chin height.
  2. Reward any touch. Hold a treat behind the towel so the dog pushes its muzzle into the fabric to reach it. Mark and reward the touch.
  3. Add the word. Once the dog pushes in reliably, say "wipe" just before it does. Practice five to ten reps a few times a day.
  4. Ask for a rub, not a touch. Reward only when the dog drags its chin across the towel, so water actually comes off.
  5. Move to the bowl. When the dog lifts its head after drinking, say "wipe" before it walks off. Reward at the towel.
  6. Fade the treats slowly. Keep paying for wipes after the big drinks, such as after walks and meals.

Some owners automate the reward with a button-activated treat dispenser under the towel. That works, but watch for a dog that learns to "wipe" over and over just to get paid. Teenage dogs learn this kind of cue quickly but test the rules. Our Cane Corso teenage phase training reset explains how to keep cues consistent through that stage.

Checklist: a lower-drool home in one weekend

  • Put an absorbent mat under the water bowl and a spare one in the wash.
  • Buy or set aside a second water bowl and start swapping instead of refilling.
  • Hang a drool towel next to the bowl, one by the back door and one in the car.
  • Start the "wipe" cue at the towel: three short sessions a day.
  • Line the crate floor with a washable bed or mat sized to the crate interior. Our Cane Corso crate size guide explains how to measure it.
  • Add a weekly "look up" clean: walls at head-shake height, door frames, cabinet fronts, screens and ceiling above the bowl.
  • Tell visitors in advance not to wear their best clothes, and keep a towel by the door for greetings.

Common drool mistakes Corso owners make

Expecting a 3 to mean "dry"

Average on the AKC scale is still a lot of water for a dog this size. Plan the house for a wet chin after every drink.

Only cleaning the floor

Head shakes throw drool upward. Dried drool on walls and screens is harder to remove than a fresh drip, so a quick weekly pass beats a big monthly scrub.

Refilling a slimy bowl

Fresh water poured over the film left by the last drink turns slimy faster. Swap to a clean bowl instead.

Wiping after the dog has walked away

By then the drool is on the floor and your trousers. The cheapest fix is timing: wipe at the bowl, or train the dog to do it.

Blaming the dog for drool on toys

Chew toys end up slimy because the dog is doing exactly what toys are for. Pick toys that can be rinsed and dried quickly. Our durable Cane Corso toy guide notes which materials hold up to heavy chewers.

Frequently asked questions

Do Cane Corsos drool a lot?

They drool a moderate amount. The AKC rates the breed 3 out of 5, or "Average", the same as the Rottweiler and Bullmastiff and less than the Mastiff, Neapolitan Mastiff and Dogue de Bordeaux.

Which mastiff breeds drool more than the Cane Corso?

On the AKC scale, the Mastiff scores 4, and the Neapolitan Mastiff and Dogue de Bordeaux score 5, compared with 3 for the Cane Corso.

Why does my Cane Corso drool so much after drinking?

The breed standard calls for upper lips that hang moderately and cover the lower jaw. Water collects in that lip pocket and drains out as the dog walks away from the bowl.

Do some Cane Corsos drool less than others?

Yes. Dogs with tight lips drip less than dogs with loose, heavy flews. Look at the parents' lips if drool matters to you when choosing a puppy.

How do I stop drool on the walls?

Wipe your dog's face before it shakes its head after drinking or eating, teach a "wipe" cue, and do a quick weekly clean of walls, doors and ceilings near the water bowl.

What is the best way to deal with slime in the water bowl?

Keep two bowls and swap them instead of refilling one, and scrub the used bowl with hot water every day.

The bottom line on Cane Corso drool

How much Cane Corsos drool depends on the dog's lips more than on the breed label. The AKC puts the breed at an average 3 out of 5, below the heavy-jowled mastiffs. For most owners the mess is concentrated at the water bowl, at mealtimes and after a head shake. A mat, two bowls, a towel at the bowl and a trained "wipe" cue take care of most of it, and a weekly look-up clean handles the rest.
